Dec Page Intelligence Agent architecture

A five-stage agentic pipeline that turns a customer's current homeowners Dec Page into a Proposed Quote with side-by-side comparison and agent talking points. Built deliberately at the workflow-with-LLM-nodes altitude, enriched with synthetic property intelligence as a strategic nod toward data-advantaged underwriting.

01 / USE CASE

The business story

A customer shopping homeowners coverage hands over their current Dec Page. The system extracts everything, layers in property intelligence, generates a competing Proposed Quote, and produces a side-by-side comparison the agent can walk through in two minutes.

Why this use case

The homeowners insurance market in 2026 is shaped by aggressive customer acquisition and agency growth across the industry. Every new quote is essentially a switch-decision transaction: a customer with existing coverage somewhere, being asked to move.

The work an agent does manually today, retyping policy data, eyeballing coverages, sketching a quote, takes 20 to 40 minutes per opportunity. Compressing it to under two minutes with a structured side-by-side is real revenue lift.

Why it's agentic, not just OCR

Extraction is only one stage. The system also reasons about coverage adequacy (is the customer underinsured against estimated replacement cost?), gap analysis (no umbrella, no scheduled property, no water backup), and property risk (synthetic property intelligence signals).

It generates a Proposed Quote rather than just extracting one. That generation step is where the agentic reasoning sits, and where the strategic value of property intelligence becomes visible.

03 / STAGES

What each stage does

Stage-by-stage detail. Each row shows the purpose, input, and output. This is the design contract for the build.

01
Classifier
Confirms the document is a homeowners Dec Page and identifies the specific policy form. HO-3 is the most common; HO-5 is premium coverage; HO-6 is condo; HO-8 is older-home actual cash value. The form determines what fields to expect downstream.
Input
Raw document text
Output
ClassificationResult { policy_form, carrier, confidence, reasoning }
02
Dec Page Extractor
Pulls all structured data from the dec page into a normalized schema. Coverages A through F, deductibles (including separate wind/hail where applicable), property attributes, discounts, premium, mortgagee, and forms/endorsements. Per-field confidence scoring on every extraction.
Input
Text + Classification
Output
ExtractedDecPage { policyholder, property, coverages, deductibles, discounts, premium, mortgagee, attributes }
03
Risk Analyzer
The agentic reasoning stage. Combines the extracted Dec Page with synthetic property intelligence data. Identifies underinsurance signals (Coverage A vs estimated replacement cost), coverage gaps (no umbrella, no scheduled personal property, no water backup, no service line), and property risk factors (roof age, electrical panel, water intrusion history).
Input
ExtractedDecPage + HomeFactorsData
Output
RiskProfile { underinsurance_flags, coverage_gaps, risk_factors, recommendations }
04
Quote Generator
Generates a Proposed Quote based on the customer's current coverage plus the Risk Analyzer's recommendations. Adjusts limits to close gaps. Adds suggested endorsements. Produces a directional premium estimate using simple multipliers (not real rate tables). Justifies each adjustment in plain language.
Input
ExtractedDecPage + RiskProfile
Output
ProposedQuote { coverages, endorsements, deductibles, estimated_premium, change_rationale }
05
Comparator / Packager
Produces the side-by-side comparison of Current Dec Page versus Proposed Quote. Tags each line as Improved, Reduced, Unchanged, Gap-Closed, or New-Coverage. Generates an agent-ready narrative summary plus a customer-facing explanation. This is the artifact the human actually uses.
Input
ExtractedDecPage + ProposedQuote
Output
ComparisonPacket { side_by_side, change_signals, agent_talking_points, customer_summary }

Design principle
Even in customer acquisition workflows, the right default is agent review, not autonomous customer-facing output. The system prepares; the licensed agent decides what to present and how. That posture matters in regulated insurance, and it's what separates production-grade AI from demo-grade AI.
07 / DECISIONS

Key design decisions

The non-obvious choices baked into this architecture, and why each is defensible. Each is a talking point for the interview.

D-01
Why a workflow, not a fully autonomous agent loop?
Anthropic's published guidance for production agentic systems is to use model intelligence inside known workflow steps rather than full autonomy. For regulated insurance with downstream customer-facing consequences, autonomy at Rung 5 or 6 is the wrong default. Agents earn autonomy per use case.
D-02
Why is Stage 4 (Quote Generator) deterministic, not LLM?
Pricing is rules-based work, not language work. Coverage limit recommendations and premium multipliers are policy decisions encoded in code. Using an LLM for them adds non-determinism without adding value. The LLM is the right tool for extraction, analysis, and narrative; deterministic logic is the right tool for quotes.
D-03
Why separate Risk Analyzer from Quote Generator?
The Risk Analyzer's job is identifying what's missing or under-covered. The Quote Generator's job is translating those signals into priced coverage adjustments. Keeping them separate means risk analysis is reusable across other workflows, and the quote logic is auditable independent of the analysis.
D-04
Why per-field confidence rather than per-document?
Real dec pages have heterogeneous reliability. The policy number is usually crisp; deductible structures can be ambiguous; discount lists are often abbreviated. Per-field confidence drives granular human review, so the agent can quickly see which specific fields need verification rather than re-reading the whole document.
D-05
Why include synthetic property intelligence data?
It demonstrates the strategic data advantage that supplemental property data creates. Most extraction systems work only from the dec page itself. Layering in third-party property intelligence lets the system reason about actual property risk, which is the genuine moat in modern homeowners underwriting. Including it in the prototype makes the architectural pattern tangible.
D-06
Why generate a Quote rather than just flagging gaps?
A flag tells the agent there is a problem. A Quote tells the agent and here is the answer. The difference in agent productivity between those two outputs is substantial. The system is more valuable when it does the next step's work, not just the diagnostic.
D-07
Why generate agent talking points as well as data?
The data is for the system; the talking points are for the human. New agents especially need narrative scaffolding to walk customers through a comparison confidently. Generating that scaffolding closes the gap between system output and customer conversation.
08 / EXTENSIONS

Production considerations and extensions

What this prototype demonstrates, what it deliberately omits, and where production-grade extensions would invest first.

What this prototype proves

That a regulated-industry agentic AI workflow can be architected with the right altitude of LLM autonomy, with structured outputs between stages, deterministic logic where rules apply, and human-in-the-loop on ambiguity. The architectural pattern is the contribution.

The five-stage chain demonstrates that LLM intelligence and deterministic logic can coexist productively in the same pipeline. Stage 4's deliberate exclusion of the LLM is as architecturally meaningful as Stage 3's inclusion of property intelligence reasoning.

What production-grade extensions would require

A real eval harness with golden datasets and continuous evaluation. Real rate tables and actuarial logic in place of directional multipliers. A live property intelligence data feed in place of the synthetic JSON. Enterprise observability infrastructure for traces, costs, and quality drift. Integration with policy administration, agent management, and CRM systems.

Each is real engineering work that wasn't in scope for a four-day prototype but maps cleanly onto the existing architecture without structural changes.

The point of a prototype
A prototype is not a smaller version of a production system. It is a demonstration that the architectural pattern is sound. The work that turns this into production is real, but it is incremental work on a foundation that already makes the right choices about autonomy, governance, and where LLMs do and do not earn their place.
